At a Thai-Isan Wedding: Part 4
Paying Respects to the Elders

Next comes paying respects to the elders. In Thai it is called kho khama, asking to be forgiven for any wrong or offence, as a young man does with his elders before becoming a monk. The couple make a krap, a deep bow with palms joined at the forehead and the head lowered to the floor, to the parents and elders of both families. They often present pha wai, gifts of cloth that show respect. In Isan, the bride traditionally prepares silk clothes and pha khao ma cloth for the groom's grandparents and older relatives. In return, the elders bless the couple. For guests, this is a family moment to watch quietly. Keep your phone on silent, and ask before taking photos. If you are invited to add a blessing, a few warm words are perfect.
