At a Thai-Isan Wedding: Part 1
The Monk Ceremony

The morning starts with monks, invited to chant blessings for the couple. In Isan tradition this often happens the evening before, at a gathering called muea hom, with a sermon on a lasting married life; here it opens the wedding day. In the form described by Thailand's Department of Religious Affairs, the couple sit before a Buddha altar, light candles and incense, bow, and receive the precepts with everyone present. Monks hold a white cotton cord, the sai sin, while they chant. The couple put food into the monks' alms bowls side by side, then offer a meal and gifts. Afterwards they pour water from a small vessel, the kruat nam, to share the merit, and the monks sprinkle holy water. Be calm and gentle around monks. Women should never touch a monk.
