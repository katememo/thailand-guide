Ban Chiang: Stop 1
A Village That Rewrote Prehistory

Welcome to Ban Chiang. The quiet farming village around you sits on top of one of the great archaeological finds of Southeast Asia. UNESCO describes Ban Chiang as the most important prehistoric settlement so far discovered in the region, with the earliest evidence there of farming and of making and using metal. The ancient site is an oval mound built up by people living on it, about five hundred metres wide, thirteen hundred and fifty metres long and eight metres high. Only a tiny fraction has been dug: UNESCO put it at less than one tenth of one percent in 2012. Its heritage advisers noted that until the 1960s, experts saw prehistoric Southeast Asia as a backwater shaped by China and India. Ban Chiang helped overturn that view, and it joined the World Heritage List in 1992.
