Ban Chiang: Stop 3
Farmers of the Prehistoric Village

In the sixth section of the main exhibition, scenes and objects recreate daily life in prehistoric Ban Chiang. So what were people doing here? UNESCO's advisers described the first settlers as hunter-gatherers who were beginning to farm, keeping cattle, pigs and chickens and growing a simple kind of dry-field rice. Later, they say, came wet-rice farming in flooded fields, revealed by the bones of water buffalo. When you step outside, look at the rice paddies around the village. UNESCO notes that after the ancient settlement was abandoned, farmers from across the Mekong River came here in the late eighteenth century and once again took up wet-rice growing, and that the landscape keeps its traditional farming character. Today's villagers are Thai Puan people, whose way of life and history fill a separate exhibition building.
