The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew: Stop 8
Dusit Maha Prasat Throne Hall

Our last stop is the Dusit Maha Prasat throne hall, built by King Rama the First. It replaced an earlier throne hall built entirely of wood, which burned down in 1789 after a lightning strike. The new hall was modelled on a throne hall in the royal palace of Ayutthaya. Look up at its seven-tiered spire, and at the four corners of the roof, where red Garudas clutch the heads and tails of serpents. When King Rama the First died, his body lay in state here, and since then the hall has been used for the lying-in-state of kings, queens and senior members of the royal family, as well as for royal ceremonies. Inside stands a throne inlaid with mother of pearl, sheltered by a nine-tiered white umbrella of state.
