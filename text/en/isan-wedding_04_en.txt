At a Thai-Isan Wedding: Stop 4
Baai Sri Su Khwan, Calling the Spirit

This is the heart of an Isan wedding, the Baai Sri Su Khwan. Thais believe each person has a khwan, a life spirit present from birth that can flee when we are frightened. Su khwan means calling it back. At the centre stands the bai si, tiers of folded banana leaf decorated with flowers, holding candles, sweets, bananas and cotton threads. Everyone sits around it, traditionally elders on the north side and guests on the south. A ritual master, the mo khwan, chants and calls, ma de khwan oei, come, khwan. Then the mo khwan ties threads on the couple's wrists. Parents, elders and guests follow, each tying a white thread with a blessing, to keep the khwan from leaving. When your turn comes, tie gently and offer a warm wish. Ask your hosts how long to wear them.
