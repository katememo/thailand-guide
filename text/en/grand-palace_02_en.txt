The Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew: Stop 2
The Golden Chedi and the Upper Terrace

Look for the great golden bell-shaped monument. This is the Phra Sri Rattana Chedi, about forty metres high, on the raised platform at the western end of the temple. King Rama the Fourth built it in 1855 to hold relics of the Buddha. It began as plain brick and plaster; its gold mosaic tiles came from Italy in time for Bangkok's hundredth anniversary in 1882, and a century later more were ordered from the same Italian company for the bicentenary repairs. Close by, the square Phra Mondop was built by King Rama the First to house the Buddhist scriptures. The building crowned by a single corncob-shaped spire is the Royal Pantheon, which holds life-size statues of the Chakri kings and opens to the public only on a few days each year, such as Chakri Day on the sixth of April.
