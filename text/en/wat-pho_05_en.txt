Wat Pho: Stop 5
A Library in Stone and the Art of Thai Massage

Between the chedis and the ordination hall stand two open pavilions. Look up along the veranda of the northern one: painted figures, marked with lines and points for massage, sit beside inscribed stone panels of instructions. They belong to a vast library in stone. Between 1831 and 1841, King Rama the Third had more than fourteen hundred inscriptions fixed to the walls, pillars and beams of some twenty-five buildings here, so that the public could read them. They cover Buddhism, literature, history and above all health: nearly half deal with medicine and massage. In 2011, UNESCO added these Epigraphic Archives of Wat Pho to its Memory of the World Register. Then in 2019, Nuad Thai, traditional Thai massage, was inscribed as intangible cultural heritage of humanity. The temple's traditional medicine school still teaches here today.
