Wat Pho: Stop 1
The Reclining Buddha

Step into this long hall in the northwest corner of the temple. Stretched out before you is Phra Buddha Saiyas, the Reclining Buddha. It is forty-six metres long and fifteen metres high from the base to the topknot, and the face alone measures five metres from hairline to chin. Under the gold, the figure is built of brick and stucco, then lacquered and gilded. King Rama the Third had it made in 1832, during his great restoration of Wat Pho, because the temple already held Buddha images in many postures but none lying down. It is the largest reclining Buddha in Bangkok. Now look at the walls between the windows and doors: the murals show the Buddha's foremost disciples, including nuns, laymen and laywomen, painted to teach as well as to inspire.
