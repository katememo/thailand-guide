At a Thai-Isan Wedding: Stop 3
The Khan Mak Procession

Next, the groom's side arrives in a procession, the Khan Mak. A khan mak is a bowl of betel leaves and areca nuts, carried with other gifts as a sign of respect to the bride's parents. In Isan the procession is joyful, with cheering and dancing, and you may hear the khaen, the bamboo mouth organ of the northeast, played at happy occasions. An elder usually leads, followed by people carrying trays and gifts, then the groom. On the bride's side, playful gates bar the way, sometimes called the silver gate and the gold gate, and the groom's side hands over small envelopes of money to pass. Inside, the bride's elders open the trays and count the sin sot, the bride price, and scatter grains and flowers over it for good luck. If invited, walk along and enjoy it.
