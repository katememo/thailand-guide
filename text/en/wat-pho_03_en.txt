Wat Pho: Stop 3
The Chinese Stone Guardians

Leave the hall and head toward the four tall, tiled chedis. Before you enter their walled courtyard, look at the gateway. Each gate is watched by a pair of Chinese stone figures, and you will meet many more around the temple. Figures like these were carried as ballast, weighing down trading junks on the voyage home from China, then set up here as decoration. The fierce armoured warriors in Chinese opera costume, holding weapons, are called lan than. Look for their quieter companions too: scholars with a fan or a book, monks with a string of beads, workers with a hoe or a net, elegant ladies, and even figures in European dress, four pairs of them said to represent Marco Polo. By the cloister stairs, stone lions keep a small round stone loose inside their open mouths.
