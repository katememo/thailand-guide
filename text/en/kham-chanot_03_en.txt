Kham Chanot: Stop 3
A Forest of Chanot Palms

Now look up. The tall trees crowding around you are chanot palms, also called taraw palms. Thai tourism descriptions say a chanot looks like a sugar palm and a coconut palm combined into one tree, and here they grow naturally and very densely. The provincial tourism office calls this a primeval forest and says these chanot trees are found here and nowhere else. The Tourism Authority of Thailand adds that the site is interesting to botanists too, describing it as a freshwater swamp forest, and calling it the only one of its kind and the largest in the country. Some official guides describe the whole forest as a floating island. Before moving on, pause for a moment and notice the deep shade and the cool, quiet air.
